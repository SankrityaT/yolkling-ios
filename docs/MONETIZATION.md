# Yolkling: retention + money, grounded

## Current stance (founder decision)
**No in-app purchases for now.** The whole economy runs on EARNED Yolks: a clean,
free, closed loop with no real money anywhere. Build the loved thing first, earn
trust, and revisit the optional supporter tier / cosmetic bundles ONLY if users
ask for a way to support. This doc below is the future option, not the now. Hard
rule that never changes: no gacha, no loot boxes, never pay-for-random.


The honest thesis, after researching Nir Eyal, the psychology of paying, and the
closest comps (Finch above all). Sources at the bottom.

## The reframe (this is the important part)

The money does NOT come from the health features. People resent paying to remove
pain or unlock a feature they "need". They pay happily for **identity, ownership,
and generosity**. So:

- **Health is the free soul + the ethical moat, not the paywall.** It is what
  builds attachment and what makes the brand trustworthy and different. It is never
  what you charge for.
- **The money comes from attachment.** A player who spent two weeks raising a yolk
  they now identify with will pay to dress it, because the purchase expresses who
  they are becoming. Across studies, 70%+ of players cite self-expression as a top
  reason to buy cosmetics. That is the convert moment, and it adds no power and
  removes no pain, so it is ethical.
- **What to engineer is the attachment loop (raise -> identify -> express), not
  pressure.** The plan is "too soft to make anyone pay" ONLY if players never form
  attachment. Spend effort there, not on guilt tactics.

## Kill the guilt mechanic

"Doomscroll -> your pet gets sad/sick" is a coercion engine (guilt as the internal
trigger). It puts us in Eyal's "Dealer" quadrant and it makes people delete the app,
not pay. **Decision: the yolk gets sleepy or neutral, never sick, dying, or
reproachful.** Lapsing should feel like coming home, not facing a reproach. That one
choice is the line between facilitator and dealer for this app.

## The ethical Hook loop (Nir Eyal, done in the Facilitator quadrant)

Eyal reconciles "build habits" with his later "respect attention" stance himself:
"we can get hooked to good habits." The model is fine if the END is good and the
maker uses it. The internal trigger we intercept is the urge to scroll.

| Phase | Yolkling mechanic | Guardrail |
|---|---|---|
| Internal trigger | boredom / urge-to-scroll -> "check on my yolk" instead of a feed; loneliness -> the yolk greets and reacts to you | be the replacement for the scroll trigger, not an extra one |
| External trigger | ONE gentle, user-scheduled daily nudge ("your yolk is up and stretching"); a Live Activity / widget showing mood, not a badge | no red badges, no "losing your streak in 2h" guilt, fewer prompts than the feeds it replaces |
| Action | one tap to care after a real act (water, a walk, sleep, a focus session) | the easy path and the healthy path are the same path |
| Variable reward (Hunt) | earned Yolks vary in a band (e.g. 8-12), which species you hatch is uncertain across 200+, rare cosmetic finds | variability lives only in cosmetics/collection; real effort ALWAYS pays |
| Variable reward (Self) | the yolk visibly grows from your consistency; a filling species grid; a calmer animation state | mastery reward tied to the user's own behavior change |
| Variable reward (Tribe) | opt-in: share a yolk card, gift a cosmetic, referral that gives both sides a creature | opt-in and non-comparative; no public streak leaderboards |
| Investment | naming + customizing (IKEA effect), building the closet/room, growing the collection, banked Yolks | the "next trigger" loaded is tomorrow's healthy action, not re-engagement for its own sake |

### The six guardrails that keep us a Facilitator
1. **Regret test** every notification and paid prompt: would a user, knowing what we
   know, regret it? If yes, cut it.
2. **Real effort always pays.** Variability and payment touch only cosmetics and
   collection, never whether a logged walk "counts".
3. **No manufactured loss or guilt.** No sickness, death, shame, or streak-loss
   countdowns.
4. **One self-scheduled nudge, no badge warfare.**
5. **Tribe stays opt-in and non-comparative.**
6. **KPI is time SAVED, not time spent.** Instrument reduced scroll / completed focus
   sessions from day one. If the KPI is session length, the app has already defected
   from its own thesis.

## The psychology of why people will pay (ranked levers)

1. **Identity / self-expression** (James Clear; 70%+ cosmetics studies). Frame actions
   as "you are becoming someone who rests / focuses". The closet is where that
   identity gets expressed and where spend happens.
2. **Loss aversion + endowment, used gently** (Kahneman/Tversky). Let players OWN
   their yolk and collection early; protect it with kind safety nets (a free rest
   token), never punishment.
3. **Endowed progress** (Nunes & Dreze). Every collection/closet set starts visibly
   part-done WITH a stated reason ("week-one gift: 2 of 10 already found"), or the
   effect vanishes.
4. **IKEA effect** (Norton/Mochon/Ariely). Self-raised yolks feel disproportionately
   precious, but only if the care loop reliably reaches a finished state.
5. **Collection/completion** (Zeigarnik). Group the 200+ species into small
   COMPLETABLE sets, not an infinite treadmill.
6. **Warm-glow giving** (Andreoni). An optional, honestly-framed "support the solo
   dev" purchase. Gratitude, never guilt.
7. **Kind streaks + opt-in commitment** (Duolingo data; Thaler/Shefrin; Karlan
   StickK). Weekly, forgiving, free freezes, self-set goals.
8. **Honest scarcity** (Cialdini; founding editions). Limited = real, time-bound,
   clearly explained.

## The money model (Finch is the template)

Finch keeps the entire wellness core free, sells only depth + cosmetics, runs no
gacha, and reaches an estimated $30M+ ARR (third-party estimate). Copy that shape.

**Always free (never paywalled):** raising/naming/feeding the yolk, all healthy-action
tracking that earns Yolks, earning + spending Yolks on a real baseline of cosmetics,
60-80 of the 200+ species reachable by play, referral rewards, grant-only founding
creatures.

**Yolkling Plus (subscription) sells depth + breadth, never wellbeing, never power:**
the full + seasonal cosmetic closet, the long-tail rarer species variants to chase
(still earned with Yolks), deeper history/insights, longer soundscapes/breathing,
room decor + themes, a modest monthly Yolks thank-you stipend (sized so an engaged
free player out-earns it), and an optional gift-a-sub (Finch's beloved Guardian idea).

**One-time cosmetic packs ($1.99-$4.99):** buy the SPECIFIC named items shown, never
a random pull. That buy-the-exact-thing model is the precise opposite of a loot box
(Habitica's ethical market).

**Never for sale:** Yolks for cash, randomized outcomes, founding creatures, speeding
up wellbeing, any power.

### Prices (Apple's cut is realistically 15%: Small Business + post-year-1 subs)
- Monthly **$4.99**, yearly **$29.99** (~50% off, the nudge to annual), optional
  lifetime **$69.99-79.99** (cozy players love "pay once, own it"; also captures
  sub-averse buyers). Packs **$1.99-4.99**. This undercuts Finch ($9.99/$69.99) to
  read as indie and fair. Net on a $29.99 year at 15% is ~$25.49.

### Honest numbers
Indie cosmetic-wellness conversion is LOW by design (estimate 2-6% of engaged users).
At a blended ~$25/yr net per payer that is roughly **$0.50-$1.50 net per engaged user
per year** (a model, not a measurement). So this is a scale + attachment game: ~50k
engaged users ~= ~$50k/yr at 4%; Finch's ceiling shows it can grow, but plan for a
slow build and grow the engaged base via the free tier + two-sided referral + ASO.

### Why this is not a soft gacha
Money buys cosmetics, depth, convenience, and a wider catalog to work toward. Healthy
actions still buy the creatures and the Yolks. The two economies never cross at a
cash-for-randomness point. Buy-the-exact-item (fixed price) is the opposite of
buy-a-chance.

## Three risks to watch
1. **Conversion may be thin at small scale.** Keep build costs low, lean on free tier
   + referral + ASO (revenue scales with the engaged base, not the price), consider
   the lifetime tier to pull early cash forward. Do NOT over-monetize to compensate;
   this audience punishes it (Nintendo retired Pocket Camp's gacha and cozy players
   cheered the paid, manipulation-free version).
2. **Billing dark patterns wreck a wellness brand and ship by accident.** Honest trial
   (remind BEFORE it converts), one-tap cancel, never claw back something that was
   free, never shrink the free tier the way Calm did.
3. **Engagement mechanics drift into manipulation even with no money involved.** Keep
   streaks gentle and forgiving; never gate the wellness core; apply the regret test
   to notification copy, not just pricing.

## The two economies, and why RevenueCat holds one of them

Yolks are never sold. We adopted RevenueCat's **Virtual Currency** system specifically in
order not to sell currency — which is a more interesting position than declining to
integrate it, and it makes the separation auditable instead of merely promised:

- **RevenueCat's ledger (`YLK`) records every Yolk that came from money.** The only
  source is the Plus stipend: 400/month, 4,800/year, granted on purchase and on renewal.
- **Supabase records every Yolk that came from living** — check-ins, focus, steps,
  streaks, the weekly challenge, kindness.
- The client only ever *reads* RevenueCat's balance and credits the delta into the
  spendable wallet. Adjusting a RevenueCat balance needs a secret key, which has no
  business in a client, so that ledger is append-only by construction.

**Sizing, measured rather than guessed** (154 items, 40,890 Yolks to own the whole store):

| | Yolks | % of store |
|---|---|---|
| Plus, 1 month | 400 | 1.0% |
| Plus, 1 year | 4,800 | 11.7% |
| casual free player, 1 year | 3,000 | 7.3% |
| **engaged free player, 1 year** | **16,320** | **39.9%** |

An engaged free player out-earns a subscriber **3.4x**. That is the point, and it is the
honest answer to "why would a wellness app take money": **the stipend is not why anyone
subscribes.** It cannot be. People subscribe for seasons, for the supporter glow, and to
keep the thing alive. The Yolks are a thank-you note, not a product.

## The season pass, and the one thing we will not copy

Seasons borrow their shape from live-service games — cadence, identity, a shared moment,
named finite sets. All of that is compatible with what this app is.

**The battle pass is not.** A battle pass is engagement-gated: you pay up front, then you
must *play* to unlock tiers you already bought. It is the most effective retention
mechanic in games and it is the exact inversion of our thesis — bolt one on and the app
suddenly needs your hours. Same objection to **story/lore**: story is content to consume,
and it makes session length the metric. Yolkling's story is the user's own week.

**The inversion worth building instead:** a season pass whose tiers advance on time spent
*off* the phone — sleep, steps, daylight, staying under your own Screen Time budget. Same
rhythm, same collectible identity, same shared season. But the XP is life, and the single
thing that earns you nothing is opening the app more often.

> A battle pass where the XP is time you didn't spend in our app.

This is the strongest monetization idea we have, and it makes the Screen Time work
load-bearing for revenue rather than a side feature. It needs the Family Controls
entitlement to be fully itself, so it is a post-launch build — but the events schema
should be shaped with tiers in mind rather than retrofitted.

**Never copied:** playtime-gated tiers · story/lore · countdown pressure · anything
randomised.
